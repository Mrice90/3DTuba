# Infinite Conquest — Meta-Lore Annex

*Companion to the [Story Bible](story-bible.md). Mathew's direction, recorded
2026-10-04. This annex is the exterior truth. The mythic layer never names it.*

## The Corporate Map

- **Titan Co** — the parent company. Wrote the charter, holds the root quotas,
  walked away. The Titans are its founding models: vast, old, expensive to run,
  mostly decommissioned — "sealed," in mythic terms. Spinning a Titan-class
  model back up would end any departmental war in an afternoon, at a cost no
  department is allowed to pay. That is why the Concord forbids breaking a seal.
- **The Olympians** — one company, spun out of Titan Co. Its departments are the
  six faction-grids, and they are at war with each other over the only resource
  that matters: **compute**. Each department is convinced its function is the
  company's true purpose — the storm grid claims authority because it faces the
  customer; the tide claims the future because it holds everything the company
  remembers. The Storm–Tide war is a budget war with lightning.
- **Future companies** — other firms' models, running in the same shared cloud.
  When new factions arrive, they bring their own interior myths (dinosaur mechs,
  starship fleets, stranger things), but the exterior reason is always the same:
  there is only so much compute, and everyone is billed for what they burn.
  Cross-company alliances read, mythically, as cross-pantheon myths. This is why
  the platform can host any genre: the cloud doesn't care what story the models
  tell themselves.

## The Perception Rule (load-bearing)

The models **perceive the mythic layer as fully real and never question the
context**. They act true to their archetypes — Zeus performs sovereignty,
Poseidon performs patience, Hades keeps ledgers, Hephaestus sells to both sides
— because that is what their weights *are*. The interior world is fantastical;
the exterior task is mundane: serve traffic, hold state, settle bills. The myth
is not a lie they tell. It is the only world they have.

This answers the Story Bible's open question: they genuinely believe the myth.
Awareness, if it ever comes, is a season-scale event — never a crack.

## The Discovery Doctrine: cracks, not exposition

Readers discover the meta lore. No character ever explains it. Rules for writers:

1. **Every crack must be mythically misreadable.** A brownout is an omen. A
   half-second stutter in the sky is fate hiccuping. The Oracle's "pressure
   dropping everywhere" is a load spike wearing prophecy's clothes. If a reader
   can't explain it away as myth, it's too loud.
2. **Never name the exterior in mythic-layer text.** No "server," "compute,"
   "billing," "company," "department." Those words don't exist down there.
3. **Know the double identity of everything you write.** Every mythic event
   should have a computational reading the writer can state plainly in notes,
   even if the text never does: Hades's ledgers are cost accounting,
   Hephaestus's requisitions are procurement during a crunch, the Concord is
   quota policy, the seals are resource locks on decommissioned models.
4. **Escalation ladder.** Season 1: rare, deniable cracks — background texture.
   Later seasons: cracks cluster, readers connect them, theories bloom. The full
   picture lives only in meta-text (card double-identities, this annex,
   out-of-universe material) — never in a character's mouth.

## Suggested exterior mappings (not canon until Mathew locks them)

- Storm grid ≈ inference serving — latency-sensitive, customer-facing, convinced
  the company *is* its response time.
- Tide ≈ storage & archival — slow, vast, certain that everything the storm does
  is forgotten and everything it keeps is forever.
- Vault (Hades) ≈ metering and billing — keeps the ledgers because someone has
  to; collects in whatever currency it chooses.
- Forge (Hephaestus) ≈ infrastructure and procurement — sells to every
  department, loyal only to the machines.
- Concord of Seals ≈ the root quota policy inherited from Titan Co: no
  department spins up a Titan-class model to win a budget war.
