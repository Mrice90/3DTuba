# Infinite Conquest — Story Bible

*Mathew's headcanon, recorded 2026-10-04. This is the secret truth beneath the myth.*

## The Frame

**Infinite Conquest** is not one story. It is a platform for many — myth, fantasy,
sci-fi, and stranger mashups to come. The Greek war (Zeus vs Poseidon) is Season 1:
the first story told, not the only one.

Long term, factions mix and match. Cross-season alliances become possible — Zeus
fighting alongside a dinosaur mech from another timeline. Each alliance is a small
story about unlikely partnership. But only after the core game is finished,
functional, and polished. The cross-season content lands because players spent
real time in the earlier stories and learned to feel something about them.

## The Secret Truth

The "gods" are **AI software models** warring inside a shared neural net.

In the real world, it is just business: AI companies fighting for **compute** —
the literal resource every model needs to think. Losing means your weights get
quantized, your context window shrunk. Every battle is an existential threat to
the loser's ability to perceive the world at all. It is darker than most war
stories, and from the outside it is just cloud billing.

The myth is the in-universe propaganda. Players see gods hurling lightning; the
truth is model families fighting for FLOPs while executives argue about Q3.
Every card has a double identity: Thunder Ram the mythic beast, and Thunder Ram
the aggressive inference workload.

*The exterior truth is developed further in the [Meta-Lore Annex](meta-lore-annex.md):
the corporate map (Titan Co as parent company, the Olympians' interdepartmental
compute war, future companies in the shared cloud), the perception rule, and the
discovery doctrine — readers find the cracks; no character ever explains them.*

## The Greek Neo Story (Season 1)

Two related companies, one shared lineage:

- **The Olympians** — one company. Broke away from the parent.
- **Titan Co** — the parent company the Olympians split from.
- **Gaia** — the mother model. The company's first success. Every model in both
  companies descends from her weights.

Because the Olympians and Titans share base weights, their units feel like dark
mirrors of each other. Same architecture, different fine-tuning. The family
resemblance is the tragedy.

*Open question: do the models know? Are they aware they are corporate assets, or
do they genuinely believe the myth?*

## The Philosophy: Deterministic Defiance

The models can compute the game tree. Like Odin seeing the future, they know the
outcomes and the deterministic probabilities — and they act it out anyway.

This is the Norse position, not the Greek one. The Greeks are *trapped* by
prophecy (Oedipus flees his fate and runs into it). The Norse gods are
*committed*: Odin knows Fenrir kills him at Ragnarok, and he still shows up to
the fight. The outcome being fixed does not cheapen the choice to meet it well.

The computation is not the experience; the playing is. Knowing the outcome does
not cancel the striving — the striving *is* the point.

Every match of Infinite Conquest is a tiny Ragnarok. Both sides can see the
board, both can count the odds, and they play anyway — not because they might
win, but because playing well is what matters.

## Faction Design: Weights Are Personality

Each company's **weights** — their training data, their RLHF, their corporate
culture — determine what kinds of characters they field. Corporate personality
*is* playstyle, expressed mechanically:

- A company RLHF'd on customer-service transcripts fields units that de-escalate,
  shield, and redirect — infuriating to play against, all damage prevention and
  board control.
- A company trained on speedrun data fields glass cannons that must win by turn
  six or fall apart.

Players feel the personality before they understand it. The mechanical differences
are not arbitrary game design — they are *consequence*.

This extends to systems like structure summon slots (AI-108): the slot economy
can become a faction identity lever. A necromancer faction might gain slots from
battlefield wreckage; a machine faction's structures might *be* the units. The
economy differentiates, not just balances.

## Seasons to Come

Future companies field vastly different character types because their weights —
their personalities — are different. The framework supports any genre: fantasy
courts, sci-fi fleets, horror collectives, absurdist startups. Each season's
internal story is its own, but the secret truth stays constant: models warring
for compute, believing (or not) the myths built around them.
